A cartoon that teaches. From one prompt.

A fox cub cuts a pizza and his bunny friend gets a tiny piece. Why it works

  1. A problem first. Frame 1 is a pizza being cut unfairly. By second 3 the bunny is about to cry and everyone wants to see it fixed.
  2. One idea only. Same size = one half. Different sizes = not halves. Nothing else competes for attention.
  3. The proof is visual. One part flips onto the other and the outlines match. The child sees it, not just hears it.
  4. Your child's name. The fox asks Mia for help and praises Mia at the end. Put your kid's name in one line.
  5. Kind, not loud. No flashing, no fast cuts, soft colors. Short lines a parent can read out loud.
  6. Change 4 lines. Topic, age, name, key idea. Page 05 has 10 ready lessons from counting to money.

one-half.prompt / 1 of 2

Create a single self-contained index.html file with a 30-second 3D educational cartoon for young kids, made in Three.js (load it from a CDN). Everything must be built in code: no external 3D models, textures or images. Do it in one go without asking me any questions, and tell me when the file is ready.

THE LESSON (change these lines to make a new cartoon)
- TOPIC: what "one half" means
- AGE: 6 years old
- CHILD'S NAME: Mia
- KEY IDEA: if you split something into 2 parts that are exactly the same size, each part is one half. If the parts are different sizes, they are not halves.

STYLE
- A warm, kind, modern preschool cartoon: soft rounded shapes, pastel colors, soft sunny lighting, soft shadows, gentle squash-and-stretch on every move.
- Kind, not overstimulating: no flashing, no loud colors, every step easy for a child to follow.
- Make it as beautiful and polished as you can.

CHARACTERS
- Pip: a small round orange fox cub (#f08a4b fur, cream #fff1df belly and muzzle, big dark eyes with white highlights, a tiny smile). About 0.6 m tall.
- Lulu: a small round white bunny (#f7f3ee fur, pink #f6b8c6 inner ears and cheeks, big dark eyes). About 0.6 m tall, ears included.
- The pizza: 0.4 m across, golden crust #e8b25c, cheese #ffd76a, 6 red pepperoni slices #d9573b.

SET (follow these numbers exactly)
- Units are meters, Y is up, the floor is at y = 0.
- A round wooden kitchen table (#c98f5a), tabletop at y = 0.75, 1.2 m across, centered at (0, 0.75, 0).
- The pizza lies in the middle of the table at (0, 0.76, 0).
- Two stools behind the table: Pip sits at (-0.4, 0.75, -0.45), Lulu's stool is at (0.4, 0.75, -0.45). Both face +Z (toward the starting camera).
- A pale mint kitchen wall (#cfe8dc) at z = -1.5 with a big window on the left showing a sunny sky and a tree, and two potted plants on the window sill.
- Warm soft sunlight comes from the window, upper left.

THE HOOK (most important)
- This cartoon must stop an adult scrolling a social feed. The first 3 seconds decide everything.
- Frame 1 is already in motion, bright and clear: the pizza fills the screen and is being cut. No title, no fade-in, no empty establishing shot.
- By second 3 the viewer must feel a small problem and want to know how it ends: Lulu got a tiny unfair piece and is about to cry.
- Make the food look delicious: glossy melted cheese, long stretchy cheese pulls every time the pizza is pulled apart.

ON-SCREEN TEXT
- Large rounded friendly letters (at least 52 px tall at 1080p), dark text #3b2f2a on a soft white rounded bubble in the lower third. Max 8 words per line, so a parent can read it out loud.
- Text fades in over 0.2 s and out over 0.2 s.

Why the prompt works

  1. A world in meters. Y is up, the water is at y = 0, every object has a position. The model stops guessing the layout.
  2. Cameras as numbers. Start, end and target for every shot, FOV 35°. Both models framed the same five shots.

  4. Colors as hex. Fur, yuzu, lantern light and sky are fixed, so the mood matches across models.
  5. One clock, 24 s. Everything runs off one timer, so the file can be rendered frame by frame without stutter.
  6. Hard to fake. Water, steam, snow and fur in one scene: where 3D from code usually falls apart.

Page 04: Make it yours

The structure is the trick, not the capybara. Keep the blocks and the numbers, swap what is in them.

Block In this prompt Yours (example)

PROP a yuzu on its head a cup of tea steaming next to it
LIGHT stone lantern #ffb46b + blue moonlight neon sign #ff4fa3 + cold street light
HARD STUFF water, steam, snow, fur rain on glass, drops, reflections
SHOTS 5 shots x ~5 s, positions in meters same 5 shots, move the numbers to your hero
BEATS blink 6.0 s, ear 7.5 s, sniff 8.5 s tail flick 6.0 s, yawn 8.0 s, slow blink 9.0 s

Render it smooth. A screen recording of a WebGL page drops frames. Render it frame by frame instead: the file runs off one clock, so time can be stepped exactly 1/60 s per frame.

  1. Serve the folder locally and open index.html in headless Chrome at 1920x1080.
  2. Before the page loads, replace performance.now and requestAnimationFrame with a fake clock.
  3. Step the clock by 16.67 ms, run the frame callbacks, take a screenshot.
  4. Repeat 1440 times for 24 s. Pipe the frames straight into ffmpeg.
  5. Same clock for every model, so side-by-side cuts land on the same frame.

Tips

  1. Empty folder each. One folder per model, so neither sees the other's file.
  2. Same effort. Both on high. A fair test changes only the model.
  3. Check the cameras. Exact numbers can hide an object: in shot 3 the lantern is out of frame, only its light shows.
